Purrsor

Purrsor puts a cat toy on the end of your mouse pointer. It keeps a few settings on your computer and nothing else.

  • No network access. Purrsor makes no internet connections. It has no analytics, crash reporting or update checks, and no account.
  • Your mouse pointer. To draw the toy, Purrsor reads where the pointer is on your screen many times a second. It doesn't record or keep it, and it doesn't see what you click or what is on your screen.
  • Keyboard shortcuts. Purrsor asks Windows for two shortcuts, Ctrl+Alt+T and Ctrl+Alt+N. Windows tells Purrsor only when one of those is pressed; Purrsor doesn't see any other keys you type.
  • Settings. Purrsor keeps your choice of toy, style, size, weight and wiggle, and whether the toy is showing, in a small settings file in your user folder. It stays on your computer.
  • Start with Windows. If you turn on Start with Windows, Purrsor adds itself to Windows' list of apps that start when you sign in. Turning it off removes it.

Buying and installing through the Microsoft Store

If you get Purrsor from the Microsoft Store, Microsoft handles the purchase, payment and download under the Microsoft Privacy Statement. We never see your payment details. Microsoft gives us reports such as sales counts by country, ratings and reviews you post publicly in the Store, and, depending on your Windows diagnostic settings, crash reports. We use these only to support and improve Purrsor.
